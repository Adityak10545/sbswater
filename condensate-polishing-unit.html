Overview

What is a Condensate Polishing Unit?

Sugar mills and distilleries generate large volumes of condensate from evaporators, pan boiling and spent-wash concentration. This water is a valuable resource, but it carries residual organics, volatile acids, dissolved solids and elevated temperature that make it unsuitable for direct reuse or discharge.

A Condensate Polishing Unit (CPU) treats this condensate to the quality required for reuse within the plant. At SBS Water & Infra Projects Pvt. Ltd., we design, supply, install and commission two types of CPU, RO-based and Biological, built around each client's condensate characteristics, flow rate and reuse application.

  • Lower freshwater intake from river or borewell
  • Reduced hydraulic and organic load on the ETP
  • A key building block for Zero Liquid Discharge

Process

How our RO-Based CPU works

Raw condensate is typically acidic (pH 3.5–4.5) and warm (37–40 °C). Each stage prepares the water for the next, so the membranes run reliably and deliver high recovery.

  1. Step 01

    Condensate Collection

    Raw condensate from evaporators / MEE is received in the collection tank, which balances flow variations.

  2. Step 02

    Cooling

    A cooling tower brings condensate from 37–40 °C down to about 32 °C, protecting the RO membranes.

  3. Step 03

    pH Correction

    Alkali dosing in the mixing tank neutralizes the acidic condensate to the required pH range.

  4. Step 04

    Flocculation & Settling

    Flocculants bind fine particles in the alkali contact tank; they are removed in the settling tank.

  5. Step 05

    Dual Media Filtration

    Clarified water passes through dual media filters, reducing TSS to below 2 ppm ahead of RO.

  6. Step 06

    Reverse Osmosis – I

    Parallel RO-I trains operate at around 67% recovery, removing dissolved solids, organics and volatile acids.

  7. Step 07

    Reverse Osmosis – II

    RO-I reject is treated again at 50–55% recovery, lifting total plant recovery to about 85%.

  8. Step 08

    Permeate & Reuse

    Combined permeate is stored for reuse; the small RO-II reject stream goes to ETP / disposal.

Process

How our Biological CPU works

For high-COD distillery condensates (dryer condensate, evaporation condensate and spent lees), our Biological CPU combines anaerobic and aerobic treatment with sand and carbon filtration, Ultrafiltration and two-stage RO.

A · Cooling & Conditioning

  1. Step 01

    Cooling Tower

    Hot condensate from the dryer, evaporation and spent lees is first cooled to suit biological treatment.

  2. Step 02

    Condensate Collection Tank

    Cooled condensate is collected here, balancing flow and quality variations.

  3. Step 03

    pH Dosing & Contact Tank

    pH dosing in Mixing Tank-2, then the contact tank, corrects the acidic condensate.

  4. Step 04

    Neutralization / CSTR Feed

    The neutralized condensate is held for steady feeding to the reactor.

B · Anaerobic Treatment

  1. Step 05

    CSTR Reactor

    Breaks down the bulk of COD and BOD without oxygen; biogas passes through a moisture trap and flame arrester.

  2. Step 06

    Tube Settler

    Settles anaerobic biomass and recycles it to the CSTR; excess sludge goes to the sludge holding tank.

C · Aerobic Treatment

  1. Step 07

    Aeration Tanks 1 & 2

    Two aeration tanks in series, with nutrient dosing, polish the remaining organic load.

  2. Step 08

    Clarifier-1

    Separates aerobic biomass; sludge returns to Aeration Tank-1, excess goes to sludge holding.

D · Tertiary Filtration

  1. Step 09

    Polymer Mixing & Tube Settler-3

    Polymer is dosed and fine solids are settled out.

  2. Step 10

    Sand & Carbon Filters

    From the filter feed tank, pressure sand and activated carbon filters remove residual solids, colour and odour.

  3. Step 11

    Chlorine Dosing

    Chlorine is dosed before the filter water storage tank to control biological growth.

E · Membrane Polishing

  1. Step 12

    Ultrafiltration (UF)

    Removes fine colloids and bacteria, protecting the RO; UF permeate is stored in its own tank.

  2. Step 13

    RO-1 & RO-2 Booster

    RO-1 removes dissolved solids and organics; RO-2 treats RO-1 reject to lift recovery. Permeate is stored for reuse; final reject goes to the reject tank.

F · Sludge & Backwash Handling

  1. Step 14

    Sludge & Backwash

    Sludge is held, dewatered on drying beds and sent to the dry sludge yard. UF and filter backwash goes to a backwash collection tank.

FAQs

Frequently asked questions

Which CPU is right for my plant, RO-based or Biological?

RO-based CPUs suit sugar excess condensate and lower-strength condensate, with a compact footprint. Biological CPUs suit high-COD distillery condensates such as dryer condensate, evaporation condensate and spent lees, where removing the organic load biologically first protects the membranes. We recommend the technology after analysing your condensate.

What is a Condensate Polishing Unit?

A CPU is a treatment system that removes residual organics, dissolved solids, volatile compounds and other contaminants from process condensate, so the recovered water can be reused within the plant instead of being discharged.

Where is condensate generated in sugar and distillery plants?

In sugar mills, excess condensate comes from juice evaporators and pan boiling. In distilleries, process condensate is produced mainly during concentration of spent wash in multiple-effect evaporators (MEE) and other thermal operations.

What can the treated condensate be used for?

Depending on the design and final quality, polished condensate can be reused for cooling tower make-up, fermentation and process dilution, boiler feed (with suitable downstream polishing) and other utility applications.

What recovery does an SBS CPU achieve?

Our RO-based CPU's two-stage RO design typically recovers about 85% of the condensate. RO-I operates at around 67% recovery and RO-II treats the RO-I reject at 50–55% recovery, minimising the reject sent to ETP.

Why is the condensate cooled and neutralized first?

Raw condensate is usually warm (37–40 °C) and acidic (pH 3.5–4.5). Cooling to about 32 °C and correcting pH with alkali dosing protects the RO membranes, improves rejection and extends membrane life.

Can you upgrade an existing CPU?

Yes. We assess existing condensate treatment systems, identify performance limitations, and provide modification, equipment replacement, capacity enhancement and optimization services.

What capacities do you handle?

We have executed CPU projects from around 300 KLPD to 4,400 KLPD, and a 6 MLD CPU is among our upcoming projects. Each plant is sized to the client's actual condensate generation.
