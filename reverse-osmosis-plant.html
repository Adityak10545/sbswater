Overview

What is Reverse Osmosis?

Reverse Osmosis (RO) is a membrane process that applies high pressure to push water through a semi-permeable membrane. Water molecules pass through; dissolved salts, organics, colour, bacteria and other impurities are rejected. The result is purified water (permeate) and a small concentrated stream (reject).

Conventional treatment, whether clarification, filtration or biological treatment, removes suspended solids and much of the organic load, but it cannot remove dissolved salts. RO is the step that does. That is why SBS Water & Infra Projects Pvt. Ltd. treats RO as the final purification stage of every plant we build.

  • Removes dissolved salts (TDS) and residual organics
  • Turns treated water into reusable water
  • Built into every CPU, WTP and ETP we deliver

Process

How our RO system works

Water from the upstream CPU, WTP or ETP is conditioned, filtered and passed through two RO stages for maximum recovery.

  1. CPU / WTP / ETP
  2. RO Feed Tank
  3. Pre-filtration / UF
  4. Dosing + Cartridge Filter
  5. HP Pump + RO-I
  6. RO-II (reject recovery)
  7. Permeate Tank
  8. Reuse: process & cooling
  • ~85–90% overall recovery
  • RO-II reject → ETP / ZLD
  1. Step 01

    RO Feed Tank

    Feed water from the CPU, WTP or ETP is collected in the RO feed tank.

  2. Step 02

    Pre-filtration / UF

    Dual media / multigrade, activated carbon and, where required, UF remove solids, colloids and organics that foul membranes.

  3. Step 03

    Chemical Dosing

    Antiscalant and pH correction chemicals protect membranes from scaling.

  4. Step 04

    Micron Cartridge Filter

    Fine cartridge filters are the final safety barrier before the high-pressure pump.

  5. Step 05

    High-Pressure Pump & RO-I

    High-pressure pumps drive water through spiral-wound membranes in SBS-fabricated housings.

  6. Step 06

    RO-II Reject Recovery

    RO-I reject is treated again in a second stage to lift overall recovery.

  7. Step 07

    Permeate Tank

    Permeate from both stages is collected in a common tank for reuse.

  8. Step 08

    Reject Management

    The small reject stream goes to ETP, suitable reuse or evaporation for ZLD.

  9. Step 09

    CIP & Automation

    Clean-In-Place restores membrane performance; PLC panels control and monitor the plant.

FAQs

Frequently asked questions

What is Reverse Osmosis?

RO uses high pressure to push water through a semi-permeable membrane. The membrane lets water pass but rejects dissolved salts, organics, colour and microorganisms, producing purified water (permeate) and a small concentrated reject stream.

Why does SBS add RO to every CPU, WTP and ETP?

Conventional treatment removes suspended solids and much of the organic load, but not dissolved salts. RO removes TDS and residual organics, turning treated water into high-quality water fit for reuse. Adding it to every plant maximizes the value of the water you treat.

What recovery can I expect?

Our high-efficiency RO designs are engineered for 85–90% recovery. In our RO-based CPU, two-stage RO (RO-I ~67%, RO-II 50–55% on RO-I reject) achieves about 85% overall recovery.

What happens to the RO reject?

RO reject is a small, concentrated stream. Depending on the plant, it is sent to the ETP, used in suitable applications, or concentrated further in evaporators for Zero Liquid Discharge.

Why is pre-treatment important for RO?

Membranes foul quickly if suspended solids, oil, high temperature or wrong pH reach them. Our CPUs, WTPs and ETPs act as RO pre-treatment, keeping feed water within membrane limits, extending membrane life and reducing cleaning.

Can you add RO to an existing plant?

Yes. We assess your existing CPU, WTP or ETP and add UF / RO stages, upgrade membranes or increase RO capacity, often without replacing the whole plant.
